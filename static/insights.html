💡What we've learnt about Austria

An atlas of every Katastralgemeinde, segmented from BEV LiDAR, BEV orthophotos, Sentinel-1/2, ESA WorldCover, Hansen and the Austrian cadastre. Each parcel gets terrain, vegetation, buildings, change and an auto-class. Here's what falls out so far.

🌳Land cover by area

Total area classified across 239 KGs · 3,350 km² · 25 object types collapsed below

Trees alone cover 1,593 km² — 48% of all classified ground. Crops add 611 km², grass 584 km². Buildings (roofs) take 87 km², roads + paths + parking another 90 km². Austria is overwhelmingly green: even in this lowland-heavy sample (most KGs are NÖ), tree+grass+crop+shrub+hedge make up ~88% of land.

3.4M individual trees, 1.6M grass patches, 352k roof segments, 47k vineyard polygons, 12k waterbodies — segmented and classified.

🏘️How parcels live

Auto-classified from per-parcel terrain, vegetation, buildings and Hansen loss

Of 188k parcels: 22% are mixed (multiple uses), 19% meadow, 11% built-up, 11% forest. 4,632 parcels are flagged as recent-clearfell or active disturbance — visible mostly in Steiermark/OÖ alpine forest. Only 82 parcels are dominated by water.

The classifier finds 5,925 "young/regenerating forest" parcels — places where Hansen-loss happened years ago and trees are now under 5m. That's a recovery signal we can track year over year.

🪓24 years of forest loss (Hansen)

Annual forest loss inside processed KGs · 1 px = 900 m²

Loss is not flat. The cluster 2018–2020 stands out: ~8,400 ha lost in three years across our processed area — the European bark-beetle/storm wave. 2024 alone: 1,175 ha. 2019 was the worst year on record at 3,400 ha. Six of the ten worst KGs are in Steiermark; Innerbreitenau (OÖ) leads with ~100 ha lost since 2000.

⛰️Where do parcels point?

Dominant aspect of each parcel · 188k parcels

Eastern slopes win. SE+E+NE = 43% of all parcels — these face the morning sun, the steepest are typically vineyards (S+SW+W = 44% of vineyard parcels) and orchards. The terrain is mostly gentle: 83% of parcels are level or nearly-level, only 0.5% are intermediately or moderately rugged. Forested fraction climbs sharply with ruggedness — from 19% on level ground to 83%+ in rugged terrain.

🏗️Buildings: 63k catalogued, 287k newly vectorised

Pitched roofs (51%) average 10.7 m and 3.6 storeys — village + farmhouse Austria. Flat roofs (49%) average 5.6 m and 1.9 storeys — sheds, garages, barns and modern multi-storey. The mode is 3 storeys (22.8k buildings); 11 buildings are estimated 11 storeys or more. The processor also discovered 286,984 new building footprints (6,552 ha) that weren't in the cadastre or have grown since the last update — most clustered around Feldkirch (VLBG), Krems (NÖ) and Vienna's leafier districts.

🏔️Earth moved

Net DTM change between 2022 and 2024 LiDAR campaigns · 239 KGs

Total disturbed volume: 106 million m³. Net volume change: −16 million m³ (Austria is shedding material on balance). The biggest cuts are in Vorarlberg's Walgau valley — St. Gallenkirch −7.8 Mm³, Nofels −3.4 Mm³, Altenstadt −2.8 Mm³ — a mix of construction, gravel pits and floodplain regrading. The biggest fills are in Kärnten: Lassach +3.9 Mm³, almost certainly a hydro reservoir or quarry refill.

🚩What flags taught us about the highest objects

372k flags raised by the rule auditor across 329k objects · 4 of 1.5M objects flagged manually so far

The auditor catches our mistakes before we report them. Three quarters of all height records are flagged as implausible — and the flags reveal a pattern: the apparent "tallest tree in Austria" at 194.8 m in KG 67501-north isn't a tree at all — it's a radio tower next to a hedge that the segmenter merged, and the auditor caught both (tree_height_implausible + hedge_likely_tree_row). Four of the next ten "137.8 m trees" are flagged mast_likely_misclassified — reception masts that the RF thinks are trees because their nDSM is high and narrow.

1,853 trees and 283 buildings carry an implausible-height flag. They're our backlog of high-confidence corrections.

🌲Tallest objects (raw vs. trusted)

Raw — top of the leaderboard

After flag filtering — what we believe

Once flagged objects are dropped, every "tallest tree" sits at exactly 50.0 m — the natural height ceiling for old-growth Austrian conifers (typically Picea abies in 80–120 yr stands). The processor's measured maximum for clean trees converges on the canopy plateau seen in BEV LiDAR national plots. Tallest believed roof: 48.6 m in Krems-Stein (KG 12171) — a riverside tower or church spire.

88,779 trees ≥ 30 m have no implausibility flag. They're our giant-tree atlas in waiting.

🌱Phenology & greenness

Mean Sentinel-2 NDVI across processed KGs: 0.68. Phenology dominance: 145 KGs are crop-dominant (high NDVI amplitude 0.18 — strong spring-green/autumn-brown), 69 are pasture (amp 0.17, smoother), only 21 are forest-dominant (amp 0.14, evergreen-flat). The auto-class "vineyard" parcels prefer SW/W exposures (44%) — exactly the canonical wine-growing aspect.

🎯Where we're confident

RF mean confidence across the fleet: 70.9%. Divergence between RF and rule-based fallback is only 3.1% on average — the two heads agree. The biggest disagreements are path → grass (117k cases, narrow tracks bleeding into vegetation) and road → grass (68k). These are now logged and mineable for retraining.

All 239 processed KGs achieve quality grade A. 100% RF-classified, 0% unclassified. The pipeline holds.
