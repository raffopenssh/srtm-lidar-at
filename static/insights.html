🪓 The 2018–2020 forest loss shock critical

Hansen GFC annual loss inside processed KGs · resampled to 1 m² from Hansen 30 m

2019 was the worst year on record — 3,400 ha lost in our processed area alone, 4× the 2001–2016 average. The shock begins in 2018, peaks in 2019, and remains elevated through 2024 (1,175 ha). This tracks the European bark beetle outbreak (Ips typographus) driven by the 2018 drought.

Disturbance parcels sit at 511 m average — 70 m higher than intact forest — squarely in spruce's altitudinal preference band. 4,812 parcels are classified as earthwork (salvage logging sites), 945 as recent clearfell.

We can now track this at individual-parcel resolution: which parcels were clear-felled, which are regenerating, which still show active loss.

⛰️ The elevation story ecology

How landscape character changes with altitude

At 200 m: diversity 1.63, flat cropland. At 700 m: diversity drops to 1.01 as forest takes over (22k trees/KG vs 3.8k). Above 1,700 m: diversity spikes back to 1.69 — alpine meadow, rock, sparse vegetation. NDVI drops from 0.80 at 700 m to 0.63 above treeline.
